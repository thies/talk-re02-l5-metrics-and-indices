What we will do
An applied introduction: roll your own index!

Develop various indices for residential real estate in Cambridge:

  • Mean and median sale prices.

  • Transaction price, date, property type, new-build status, tenure and address fields.

Why a raw average can mislead
Quality changes or price changes?

Suppose property prices are unchanged:

YearDetached salesFlat salesMean priceMedian price
Year A1 × £500,0009 × £300,000£320,000£300,000
Year B9 × £500,0001 × £300,000£480,000£500,000

The mean rises 50% and the median 67%, with no price inflation at all. The mix of homes sold changed.

Mean, median and composition
Medians are less influenced by outliers.

  • Median prices reduce sensitivity to extreme transactions.
  • Neither mean nor median adjusts for the types or locations of homes sold.
  • Compare levels and annual changes, then inspect the composition of the sample.

Rebase before comparing levels
Same starting value for better comparison.

Set the first year to 100:

\(\text{Rebased index}_t=100\times\dfrac{\text{price measure}_t}{\text{price measure}_{\text{base}}}\)

Different series can share a starting point while giving different annual changes.

Composition changes through time
The mix of homes sold shifts from year to year.

The share of flats, terraces, semi-detached and detached sales varies by year.

If the mix changes, unadjusted prices combine market movement and changing sample composition.

The hedonic idea
Hedonic regressions (nothing to do with hedonism)

A dwelling is a bundle of observable attributes.

  • Property type, tenure and new-build status.
  • Floor area, bedrooms and finer location measures where available.
  • Estimate the contribution of attributes and time periods jointly.

The Land Registry data have limited attribute detail; the model can adjust only for fields it contains.

Time-dummy regression
Quality-adjusted price change relative to a base year

\(\ln(P_{it})=\alpha+\sum_k\beta_kX_{ik}+\sum_t\gamma_tD_t+\varepsilon_{it}\)

  • Dependent variable: log sale price.
  • \(X\): observed dwelling characteristics.
  • \(D\): time-period indicators; one base period is omitted.
  • \(\gamma_t\) estimates quality-adjusted log price change relative to the base.

sales$year <- format(sales$date, "%Y")
sales <- subset(sales, propertytype != "O" & price > 0)

Estimate and inspect the model
Log price on observed characteristics and year

hed <- lm(log(price) ~ propertytype + new + tenure + year,
          data = sales)
summary(hed)
  • The omitted year is the reference period; with these factor levels it is the first year.
  • Coefficient signs and sizes describe conditional price associations.
  • The model controls only for observed fields included in the specification.

Turn time coefficients into an index
The base-year coefficient is normalised to zero.

Normalise the base-period coefficient to zero:

\(I_t=100\exp(\hat{\gamma}_t),\quad I_{\text{base}}=100\)

years <- levels(sales$year)
b <- coef(hed)
year_terms <- grep("^year", names(b), value = TRUE)
gamma <- c(0, b[year_terms])
hedonic_index <- data.frame(
  year = years,
  index = 100 * exp(as.numeric(gamma))
)

Example: \(\hat{\gamma}_{2017}=1.689\), so \(I_{2017}=100\times e^{1.689}\approx541\). With factor coding, confirm the omitted year and coefficient names in your fitted model before relying on extraction order.

Compare the series
Estimates not too different? Sample is relatively homogeneous?

  • Unadjusted series reflect the homes sold in each period.
  • The hedonic series aims to hold included characteristics constant.
  • Differences reveal the influence of composition and model specification.

Compare annual returns too
Diff. in 2002/03, 2008, 2012, 2021... Quality differences matter!

Return from an index level: \(\left(\dfrac{I_t}{I_{t-1}}-1\right)\times100\).

Small differences in index levels can translate into meaningful differences in measured annual growth.

Repeat-sales index
Hedonic regressions cannot capture all quality details!

  • Often only address, price and date are known—no hedonic characteristics.
  • Focus on pairs of transactions of the same building
    • Location unchanged; quality assumed unchanged (capex & depreciation?); any price change is a true time trend.
  • Drawback
    • Wastes data: buildings sold only once are discarded.
    • Is the sample representative? Starter homes trade more often—overrepresented in the index.

Repeat-sales index
Putting the intuition into formulas...

  • Hedonic price given as function of time (Y) and hedonics (X): $$ \tiny \ln( Price_{i, t1} ) = \alpha + \sum_{k=1}^{k=K} \beta_kX_{k,i} + \sum_{y=1995}^{y=2021} \gamma_kY_{y,i} + \epsilon_i $$
  • Difference between two prices at time $t_2$ and $t_1$ is difference between hedonic equation: $$ \tiny \ln(Price_{i, t2}) - \ln(Price_{i, t1}) = ( \alpha + \sum_{k=1}^{k=K} \beta_kX_{k,i} + \sum_{y2=1995}^{y2=2021} \gamma_kY_{y2,i} + \epsilon_{i, t2}) - ( \alpha + \sum_{k=1}^{k=K} \beta_kX_{k,i} + \sum_{y1=1995}^{y1=2021} \gamma_kY_{y1,i} + \epsilon_{i, t1}) \\ \tiny = \sum_{y2=1995}^{y2=2021} \gamma_kY_{y2,i} + \epsilon_{i, t2} - \sum_{y1=1995}^{y1=2021} \gamma_kY_{y1,i} - \epsilon_{i, t1} \\ \tiny = \sum_{y2=1995}^{y2=2021} \gamma_kY_{y2,i} - \sum_{y1=1995}^{y1=2021} \gamma_kY_{y1,i} - \mu_{i, t2,t1} $$
  • All constant terms drop out, the "pure" time trend remains.

Interpretation and limitations
What the index does—and does not—measure

  • Quality adjustment reduces one source of composition bias; it does not make the sample representative by itself.
  • Unobserved condition, renovation, micro-location and transaction selection can still matter.
  • Results depend on period frequency, sample rules and regression specification.
  • A residential Cambridge index is not a commercial property valuation or a total-return series.

Take-away
A price index is an estimate built from transactions, sample rules and a model.

  • Mean and median prices can move even when like-for-like prices do not.
  • Observed characteristics help control for quality differences.
  • Time-dummy coefficients become a price index.
  • This is one way of modelling the data: use the code and adapt it.
